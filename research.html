Research

We use computer simulations and scientific machine learning to investigate a broad range of soft matter problems including charge regulation effects and phase separation dynamics.

Using Machine Learning to Accelerate Charge Regulation Simulations

Charge regulation simulations combine molecular dynamics with Monte Carlo (CR-MC) moves that let ionizable groups gain or lose charge, but this charge-update step is computationally expensive. We developed charge regulation machine learning (CR-ML), a hybrid approach in which a trained model performs this step, accelerating simulations by more than an order of magnitude.

Charge Regulation Effects in Complex Systems

Nanoparticles, polyelectrolytes and other soft matter building blocks carry charges that adjust to pH, salt and their surroundings. Using hybrid molecular dynamics/Monte Carlo simulations, we study how charge regulation reshapes interactions and structure in complex systems, such as polyelectrolyte-mediated interactions between nanoparticles, work featured on the cover of Macromolecules.

Data-Driven Surrogate Models for Phase Separation Dynamics

Phase separation and domain growth are multiscale processes that are costly to simulate over long times. We develop physics-guided, data-driven surrogate models, including graph neural networks and physics-constrained neural surrogates, that learn phase-ordering dynamics from simulation data, reaching longer timescales while preserving conservation laws.
